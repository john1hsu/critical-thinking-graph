# Philosophy

Philosophy is the rational, methodical and abstract examination of reality as a whole and of the basic dimensions of human experience: what exists, what we can know, how we ought to live and what makes reasoning good.[^s1] The word comes through Latin from the Greek *philosophia*, "love of wisdom".[^s1] Its branches include logic, epistemology, ethics, metaphysics and aesthetics, plus a "philosophy of" for many other disciplines, such as science, language, mathematics, law and mind.[^s1] For critical thinking, philosophy is the parent discipline: logic and epistemology, which supply the standards for judging arguments and evidence, are among its branches.[^s1]

## Origin

Ancient tradition credits Pythagoras with the name. Diogenes Laertius, citing the historian Sosicrates, says that when Leon, ruler of Phlius, asked Pythagoras who he was, he answered "a philosopher".[^s3] In the same story Pythagoras compares life to the great festival games: some come to compete for prizes and some to trade, but the best come to watch, just as the philosopher seeks truth rather than fame or gain.[^s3] The Loeb edition's notes trace the anecdote back to Heraclides of Pontus, writing long after Pythagoras, so it is a late and possibly idealised report rather than firm history.[^s3] Britannica also points out that philosophical inquiry is central to the intellectual history of many civilisations, with major non-Western schools such as Confucianism.[^s1]

## How it works

Philosophy works mostly by argument and conceptual analysis rather than by experiment. The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on metaphilosophy (the study of what philosophy is and how it should be done) shows that philosophers disagree about the method itself.[^s2] Russell and the early Wittgenstein made the logical analysis of propositions the starting point. Ordinary-language philosophers such as Ryle and Austin analysed everyday language instead. The later Wittgenstein treated philosophy as therapy for confusions that arise when we misread how language works.[^s2] Pragmatists such as Peirce, James and Dewey tested ideas by their practical effects and treated inquiry as problem-solving. Rawls's reflective equilibrium moves back and forth between considered judgements about cases and general principles until the two fit together.[^s2] Quine's naturalism holds that philosophy is continuous with science, and experimental philosophers now test the intuitions that philosophers use as evidence. They have found that those intuitions vary more than was assumed.[^s2]

Here is how this works in a workplace. A team debates whether a new hiring screen is "fair". A philosophical approach does not start with a survey. It asks what "fair" means: equal treatment, equal outcomes or equal chances. It tests each definition against clear cases, looks for counterexamples and states the premises that each position needs.[^s2] The result does not settle the empirical facts. It does make clear what evidence would bear on the dispute and where the real disagreement lies.[^s2]

## Evidence

Philosophy is a normative discipline, so its claims are not tested like effects in psychology, but teaching it has been studied. In a 2015 randomised trial in 48 English primary schools, Philosophy for Children (one year of teacher-led philosophical dialogue) produced small gains in reading (effect size about +0.12) and maths (about +0.10). The effect on a cognitive abilities test was close to zero (+0.07). The evaluators rated the result as moderately secure.[^s4] A 2025 meta-analysis of 30 studies found an overall effect of 0.65, and 0.89 for studies that measured critical thinking. These pooled estimates are larger than the trial's and come from studies of mixed design, so read them with caution.[^s5][^s4]

## Connections

Philosophy is the field that **metaphilosophy** studies. That reflexive inquiry asks what philosophy is for and how it should proceed.[^s2] Tradition says **Pythagoras** introduced the name, but the attribution rests on late anecdote and is recorded here with low confidence.[^s3] The discipline is **applied in critical-thinking education**, most visibly through Philosophy for Children programmes, though measured gains vary by study design.[^s5][^s4] Its modern self-understanding was **influenced by Ludwig Wittgenstein**, whose early analytic and later therapeutic views shaped much twentieth-century debate about philosophy's method.[^s2] Its branches **logic**, **epistemology**, **philosophy of science** and **philosophy of language** each have their own node.[^s1] **Experimental philosophy** tests the intuitions on which philosophical arguments often rely.[^s2]
