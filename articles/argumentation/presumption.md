# Presumption

A presumption is a status a claim can hold in an argument: it may be accepted for the time being without proof, and whoever wants to reject it carries the burden of showing why.[^s1] Godden and Walton describe it as a modal qualifier on a claim rather than a property of the claim itself, which is why the same statement can be presumed in one setting and need full support in another.[^s1] A presumption does not say the claim is certain or even probable. It says where the work of argument lies, and it can always be overturned by enough contrary reason.[^s1]

## Origin

Nicholas Rescher traces the idea to the law of evidence in classical antiquity. From there it passed into medieval disputation and debate and later into the theory of knowledge, where it became a tool for reasoning under uncertainty.[^s3] In rhetoric, Richard Whately's account set the terms for later work. For him a presumption occupies the ground until something sufficient is said against it, placing the burden on objectors, and it is answered by a counter-presumption. His examples included presumptions for existing institutions and against paradox, and a deference owed to recognised experts.[^s1] Twentieth-century theorists then split the idea in several directions. Rescher tied it to plausibility and the normal course of events, Edna Ullmann-Margalit treated it as a rule of practical reasoning, and Kauffeld grounded it in the obligations speakers take on when they make claims.[^s1][^s4]

## How it works

Godden and Walton break the idea into four parts.[^s1]

- **Nature.** A presumption is a relation between a claim and a dialogue, not a feature of the claim alone.[^s1]
- **Function.** It shifts a local burden of proof onto the challenger. That reverse burden is usually only a burden to produce some evidence, not to win the whole case.[^s1]
- **Foundation.** Each presumption needs a reason, which may be epistemic, moral, procedural, social or prudential.[^s1]
- **Force.** How hard it is to rebut depends on that foundation and on how well it fits the case at hand.[^s1]

Many presumptions run on a rule plus a triggering fact: given that P holds, presume Q unless there is reason to think otherwise.[^s1] Consider a facilities team. A ticket says a meeting-room screen is faulty, and the team's rule is that a ticket filed through the help desk is presumed accurate. The technician therefore books a replacement without first proving the screen is broken. If the requester later writes that the screen just needed a different cable, the presumption falls, and the question of whether to replace the screen is decided on the evidence like any other.[^s1]

Rebutting a presumption usually takes less than proving the opposite. Godden and Walton, adapting work by Henry Prakken, list four routes: deny the triggering fact, challenge the rule itself, show that an exception applies, or bring evidence against the presumed claim directly.[^s1] When a presumption is defeated, the burden resets and the claim is weighed on the balance of considerations, though any facts that triggered it still count as evidence.[^s1]

## Kinds of presumption

Lewiński sorts the literature into three groups.[^s2] Framework presumptions make conversation possible at all, such as the presumption that a speaker is sincere. Formal presumptions are general rules of inference, including legal rules such as treating someone not proven guilty as innocent. Material presumptions are conclusions settled earlier that later arguments can use as premises without re-arguing them.[^s2] Rescher and Freeman also distinguish natural presumptions, which arise in ordinary reasoning, from conventional or stipulated ones laid down by an institution, as with statutory rules on who is taken to have survived a common disaster.[^s2]

## How to apply it

1. **Name the presumption and its ground.** Ask why this claim gets default acceptance here. An unexplained "everyone knows" is not a ground.[^s1]
2. **Check the fit.** A presumption grounded in normal conditions weakens when conditions are visibly abnormal.[^s1]
3. **Ask what would rebut it.** Agree in advance whether a doubt is enough or whether contrary proof is needed, since the required standard rises with the presumption's strength.[^s1]
4. **Watch the speech act.** Kauffeld argues that presumptions bear differently on someone who accuses and someone who proposes a course of action, and warns against importing legal rules wholesale into policy deliberation.[^s4]

## Limitations

The concept has no settled definition. Godden and Walton describe the field's picture of presumption as heterogeneous, and they criticise Whately's version as resting on common sense without a clear foundation.[^s1] Lewiński goes further, arguing that presumption may be a derivative notion that reduces to the principle of charity, Gricean maxims, speech-act conditions and inference warrants, and he resists the claim that every argumentation-scheme warrant is a presumption rule.[^s2] Users should also keep presumptions apart from bare assumptions: a presumption needs a ground that can be stated and challenged in public.[^s2]

## Connections

- **Explained by the burden of proof.** Presumption is defined by its effect on who must argue: it moves a local burden onto the party who rejects the claim.[^s1]
- **Studied in argumentation theory.** Whately, Rescher, Ullmann-Margalit, Kauffeld, Freeman, Walton and Godden all treat it as a topic in the theory of argument and dialogue.[^s1]
- **Formalized by defeasible reasoning.** Godden and Walton model presumptions as default rules with raising and defeating conditions, so presumptive inference is a form of defeasible inference.[^s1]
- **Applied in legal reasoning.** Legal systems use stipulated presumptions, such as the presumption of innocence, that stay in force across cases even when a particular defendant is convicted.[^s2]
- **Contrasts with assumption.** Unlike an assumption, a presumption must have publicly accountable grounds.[^s2]
- **Challenged by *Argumentation Theory Without Presumptions*.** Lewiński's 2017 paper argues the concept may be redundant once its parts are explained by more basic notions.[^s2]
